# Fundamentals

Let's start with a review of aerodynamic fundamentals. The experienced (or uninterested) reader can skip this subpage.

To begin, <mark style="color:blue;">aerodynamics</mark> generally refers to the flow of air, although this term is pretty loosely defined. In our case, this flow is <mark style="color:blue;">external</mark> as the air travels over the exterior of the car. Air is considered a <mark style="color:blue;">fluid</mark>, a substance that deforms continuously under an applied shear force. This is unlike solids, which resist applied shear forces by static deformation. Air is also generally considered a gas (duh — but liquid air technically does exist!), a fluid in which the molecules are widely spaced and share only brief interactions. Gases take the shape of their container, expanding to fill the entire volume.

Some important variables in aerodynamics include:

* Pressure (P) - normal force per unit area due to molecular motion
* Dynamic Pressure (q) - ($$q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$$) kinetic energy per unit volume and showing how hard the wind punches the vehicle
* Density (ρ) - mass per unit volume
* Temperature (T) - The average kinetic energy of the air molecules
* Viscosity ($$\mu$$) - Fluids internal resistance to flow which generates shear friction along the skin
* Kinematic Viscosity ($$\nu$$) - is viscosity divided by density
* Reynolds Number (Re) - Tells us whether the flow is smooth and orderly or chaotic and turbulent

Boundary Layer

* As air sweeps over the body work it interacts with the surface forming a boundary layer. This is a thin layer right next to the vehicles skin where the friction forces of the skin slow the air down from the free stream velocity to a stop at the wall.

Bernoulli Principle & Continuity

* Air has to follow conservation of mass and energy. When air speeds up over a curved surface the static pressure drops and vice versa. The pressure difference across an aerodynamic body is the cause of the aerodynamic forces.

Downforce and Drag

* Downforce (Negative Lift): When shaping the bodywork speed up the air underneath more compared to the top of the car. This creates a low pressure below the high pressure above the car. This pushes the vehicle down into the pavement which increases grip through corners without adding more mass

Drag: A penalty of pushing an object through air. It is a combination of:

* Skin Friction Drag: Friction caused by air dragging across the vehicles outer skin.
* Form or Pressure Drag: Resistance caused by pressure differences between the front and rear of the vehicle.
* Induced Drag: Extra drag created as a direct side effect of generating downforce and lift

Flow separation

* If the air moving over a body encounters too sharp of a bend or too steep of a pressure increase. It loses energy and can no longer follow the surface contour. The boundary layer breaks away which creates a turbulent wake of dead air behind it. This causes a spike in drag and large drop in downforce

Why this all matters?

Understanding these fundamentals will help the team make the correct and best decisions.

* Surface Finish = Performance: A rough lay-up, poorly prepped gel coat, exposed weave, or misaligned panel gap can prematurely break the boundary layer into turbulence or full flow separation. Even lots of small bumps can have large drag penalties.
* Structural loads = Aerodynamic loads: Every pound of downforce generated by car is transferred through the composite laminates, mounting tabs, and sandwich cores. If we don't understand where the high pressure and bending moments act there is a risk of building parts that flex out of shape under the aerodynamic load which can causes failures.
* Smart Material Placement: Aerodynamic loads demand high stiffness to weight ratios. knowing where aerodynamic pressures are highest allows us to optimize ply orientation, carbon fiber selection and reinforcement where its needed without adding unnecessary weight.
