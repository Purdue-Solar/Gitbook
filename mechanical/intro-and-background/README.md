## What do we need to make?

Brakes is a safety critical system whose main function is to transfer mechanical rotational energy to heat energy by use of brake calipers pressing brake pads into the brake rotor, slowing the car down.

* For our car, the minimum deceleration requirement is 4.72 m/s^2, required by ASC. Additionally, the front and rear braking systems need to be separate and the rear brake needs to be capable of holding 15% of the cars mass.
* Additionally, we are responsible for the parking brake. Here, the parking brake needs to hold 10% of the car's mass, but ideally we would like it to hold more like 40% of the car's mass so the car can be held on a hill without much issue

### System

Solar cars generally use hydraulic brakes, which you will nearly always find on commercial cars. Hydraulic fluid is stored in a reservoir and fills hydraulic tubes called brake lines, which connect from the foot pedal assembly to the wheel assembly. When the driver presses on the brake pedal, there is a master cylinder which converts the force from the driver's foot into a pushing of the hydraulic fluid. The brake lines carry this force into the brake calipers, which will pinch the large metal discs called rotors on each wheel and thus slow the car.

## Glossary of Terms

Brake caliper - device with pistons that push on the brake rotor with a big hydraulic pinching force

Brake rotor - large metal disc attached to the hub, which is pinched to stop the car

Brake pedal - foot operated device found in commercial cars that the driver presses to activate the brakes.

Brake pad - inside the calipers, they have low friction coefficients and are the component that touches the rotor&#x20;

Effective rotor radius - The distance from the center of the pad to the center of the rotor
