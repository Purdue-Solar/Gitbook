# Components

#### Brackets

There are three brackets that attach rear suspension to the chassis. There's a bracket attaching the top of the shock ("top bracket"), the side of the trailing arm that holds the wheel ("wheel side bracket"), and the side-arm of the trailing arm ("rear bracket").

<figure><img src="../../../../.gitbook/assets/Screenshot 2026-09-12 111912.png" alt="" width="327"><figcaption><p>nice</p></figcaption></figure>

If you look closely, the brackets are all a little different - the wheel side bracket is a bit wider than the rear bracket and the top bracket has its tabs rotated 90 degrees.

#### Trailing Arm

The trailing arm is the main component of rear suspension. It holds the motor and wheel assembly and attaches them to the chassis through the brackets. The trailing arm is able to pivot so that the shock can compress when the car goes over bumps. It also has brake caliper brackets above and below it, bolted on, that hold the calipers at the right spot around the rotor (brake disc).

The trailing arm is a very large piece of metal that is machined starting from one giant block. It takes a very long time to machine and there are many potential improvements to make surrounding manufacturability.

#### Caliper Mounting Brackets

<figure><img src="../../../../.gitbook/assets/Screenshot 2026-09-12 114114.png" alt="" width="347"><figcaption><p>top and bottom caliper mounts</p></figcaption></figure>

Brake calipers bolt onto the caliper mounts. The top caliper mount holds a main brake caliper, and the lower mount holds the parking brake caliper.

#### Shock

Similar to front suspension but with a higher spring rate.
